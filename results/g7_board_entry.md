## W7 (Q-INSPIRED) — quantum-inspired CLASSICAL optimizers for team / feature->agent assignment (g7_qinspired)

Scope and honesty: everything is classical. SA, parallel tempering and a "QAOA-style" layered cost/mixer schedule are
ordinary stochastic local search; no quantum hardware, no quantum speedup, nothing from ruvnet's QVM is used. Files (all mine):
src/bin/g7_qinspired.rs, src/bin/g7_common/{mod,world,solve,pols}.rs, results/g7_qinspired*.{json,log}, this file. No src/*.rs touched, no new crates, no commits.

### What was built
* Solvers (solve.rs, 4 unit tests incl. SA/PT/QAOA vs exhaustive on 40 random QUBOs, incremental-delta checks): SA, PT (6 replicas), QAOA-style (6 shots x 6 layers, linear-ramp inverse temperature, sin^2(beta) mixer), plus ablation solvers exact (exhaustive) and greedy (forward add + swap polish).
* Part A, team assignment: choose a K-subset x in {0,1}^N per task. Optimizer = ONLINE learned QUBO surrogate (b0 + sum_i (g_i + mean_f H[f][i]) x_i + sum_{i<j} J_ij x_i x_j, NLMS from team reward only, keyword-lead prior, count-based optimism, eps swap) + a solver. Ablations isolate surrogate vs solver: same surrogate with exact / greedy; additive-only surrogate (`qadd`, no J) with greedy.
* Part B, feature->agent weights: one-hot binary matrix Z[f][a] re-solved every 20 observations (Potts objective: traffic-weighted value - lam*sum_a (load_a - cap)_+^2) with SA/PT/QAOA, plus greedy-polish and decoupled argmax (= the exact optimum when lam=0) ablations.
* Fair baselines (as asked): keyword, eps-greedy, pooled-thompson, hebbian-robust, warm(hebr), guarded-safe (= defaults::recommended_router). Teams: each wrapped as K sequential selects with every member credited the team reward (`seq:`), PLUS the real NeuralMesh (leader router + learned co-activation edges, team_size=K) with guarded-safe and hebbian-robust leaders (`mesh(...)`). Baseline grids = the repo's standard (12 eps, 4 pts, 12 warm); each family tuned PER WORLD on seeds 1-8 (generous). "Best fair baseline" per world is picked on the EVALUATION set (conservative). Optimizer config: ONE pooled config across a part's worlds, tuned on 1-8 (conservative); a second run tunes it per world (as generous as the baselines).
* Worlds (fixed before any policy ran; world.rs header): A-sep (additive skills = trivially separable control), A-cover (complementary skills, coverage by team union), A-cover-L (24 agents, K=5), A-syn (weak singles + hidden global pairwise synergy = planted QUBO, N=12,K=4), A-syn-drift (half the synergy pairs change at T/2), A-syn-L (N=30,K=6, C=593775 teams); B-sep (kappa=0 separable control), B-cong (agent overloaded when >6 of last 20 tasks: success x max(.15, 1-.15*excess)), B-cong-drift. Reward = Bernoulli driven by one pre-drawn uniform per task (common random numbers). Oracle p, random-team p and (Part B) a cheating static-balanced-assignment ceiling are printed as references only.
* Protocol: tune seeds 1-8; held-out 31-60 (`--set held`), fresh 61-90 (`--set confirm`), frozen configs; paired 95% t-CI vs best fair baseline; Holm over all arm x world cells (49 cells, primary metric = overall success; late-quarter metric reported separately).
* Budget: every arm makes the same number of decisions (1500-3000/world). Compute is reported per arm: optimizer move-evaluations/decision and measured ns/decision (single-thread timing pass, seed 31 / first eval seed, wall clock, includes select+observe). Budget-matched solver arms use 300 evals (team) / 400 evals per solve every 20 decisions = ~20/decision (assignment). A SA iteration sweep (10..3000 evals) is in the logs.

### Overall answer to the brief
* Quantum-inspired solvers (SA / PT / QAOA-style) do NOT earn a place: they win only where the world is a planted QUBO that I built to match the surrogate (A-syn family, one independent generator), they tie with exact/greedy there at 3-10x the cost, they are marginal/non-reproducible in the mismatched coverage generator, they hurt the separable control, and Part B has no win. Rule "reject if no win in >1 world": REJECTED.
* Honest positive that is not quantum-inspired: a learned set-level surrogate (regression on member indicators, optional pair terms) with a trivial greedy solver beats the repo's mesh and sequential routers by +0.12..+0.17 in synergy worlds and +0.03 in coverage-L, at mesh-like cost; costs ~1-3 points on additive worlds. Needs real-data validation before anything is wired (all worlds are mine).
* Budget accounting: see ns/decision and evals/decision columns in the logs; no arm was given more decisions than the baselines; optimizer arms use 20-300 solver evaluations per decision that baselines do not have (this favours the optimizer, and it still does not win over its own greedy ablation).
